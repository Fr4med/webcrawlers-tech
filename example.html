TWO REAL PROJECTS · SEPTEMBER 2026

What we changed.
What we checked.

A business owner asks, “How can people find my website?” A filmmaker wants people to discover their work. These two projects show the practical website repairs and checks we start with.

WEBCRAWLERS / 29 SEPTEMBER 2026

Fix a broken contact link across the site.

Live repair checked

Before

Our crawler fetched 14 pages. Each linked to an email-protection URL that returned a 404 error without JavaScript. A contact path that looked fine in a browser created broken links for that crawler.

After

We disabled the email rewrite for this site and kept ordinary email links. The same 14-page crawl then returned no page errors or findings under the selected technical checks.

How we checked it

  • Saved the original crawl and the failing URL.
  • Repeated the crawl against the published site after the repair.
  • Checked all 15 public pages after adding our AI visibility audit page: no page errors or selected audit findings.

We also clarified our offer and added a page answering “How do I make my website visible in AI search?” A separate Google Search Console check on 30 September reported that audit page as submitted and indexed.

Indexing is a separate observation. We have not established that these changes increased rankings, AI citations, traffic or enquiries.

ARTHUR KHITRIK / 30 SEPTEMBER 2026

Give a film portfolio clear page addresses and a sitemap.

Live repair checked

Arthur's portfolio presents skate films and camera work. The site needs to explain what each page is and give search systems a consistent route to the work.

Before

The homepage had no canonical URL, which identifies its preferred address. The sitemap address returned a 404 error.

After

We added a canonical URL to each of the five public routes, published a sitemap listing those routes, and referenced it from robots.txt.

How we checked it

  • Verified the five canonical URLs on the deployed site.
  • Retrieved the sitemap and confirmed all five public entries.
  • Checked the sitemap reference in robots.txt and the site's page, redirect, asset and not-found behavior.

These checks passed on the public site at 12:33 UTC on 30 September. Visit Arthur's portfolio ↗ or inspect its sitemap.

The discovery repair is verified. Search indexing, AI recommendations and new bookings still need their own measurements.

How we measure search and AI visibility

Search engine optimization (SEO) helps search systems access and understand a website. Generative engine optimization (GEO) also considers how AI answers find, cite and describe a business. We save the questions, answers, sources and dates so later checks can be compared.

In the WebCrawlers baseline, five English buyer questions were asked once in each of ChatGPT and Claude on 29 September. None of the ten answers mentioned WebCrawlers. That small sample gives us a starting record; it is not a market ranking or evidence of a later improvement. Arthur has no comparable AI-answer baseline in these case studies.

We track crawl checks, indexing, AI mentions, visits and enquiries separately. A successful website repair is useful evidence of work completed; a business result needs additional evidence.
