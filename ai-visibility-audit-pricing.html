How much does an AI visibility audit cost in 2026?

By Adam Krestol · Published 2026-08-13

A one-time AI visibility audit costs anywhere from free (as a lead magnet, including ours) to $2,000+ as an agency deliverable. Ongoing monitoring tools run $29–$500 per month, enterprise platforms start around $499 per month, and full-service agency projects commonly land between $10,000 and $50,000. The spread is that wide because "audit" means three different products depending on who is selling it. Here is how to tell them apart, with real 2026 prices and sources.

The three things people call an "AI visibility audit"

  1. A snapshot report. Someone runs your brand through AI assistants, records whether you appear, and sends a document. Useful for awareness; changes nothing by itself.
  2. Monitoring. Software tests AI answers on a schedule and charts your citation share over time. This is what most $29–$500/month tools sell — measurement, not repair.
  3. Audit + implementation. The findings actually get fixed: crawler access, structured data, sitemaps, content gaps. This is where agencies charge thousands — and where automation can change the economics.

Two pricing mechanics to watch: most tools charge by prompt volume, so a cheap plan with too few tracked questions quietly under-measures you; and per-engine or per-seat fees can push the real bill to 2–3× the advertised price.

What a real audit must include, at any price

  • Crawler access check per AI bot — can GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended actually fetch your pages? Many sites block them by accident.
  • Answer testing on your buyers' real questions — not generic brand searches, but "who should I hire for X near Y" phrasing, with the full answers and cited sources recorded.
  • Accuracy review — what AI currently claims about your prices, services and location, and where it is wrong.
  • A prioritized fix list — findings ranked by impact, separated into "safe to automate" and "needs a human decision".
  • A baseline — timestamped numbers, so any provider's later claims of improvement can be checked against something.

Red flags that mean you're overpaying

  • Guaranteed rankings in ChatGPT. AI answers are probabilistic and controlled by third parties; nobody can guarantee placement. A provider who promises it is guessing with your money.
  • A composite "AI score" with no explanation of what was measured. Insist on stage-by-stage numbers.
  • Monitoring sold as improvement. A chart of your invisibility is not a fix. Ask exactly who implements the findings, and what it costs.
  • No rollback story. If someone will change your website, ask what happens when a change goes wrong. "We're careful" is not an answer; backups and tested undo are.

Limitations of this guide

Prices were collected in August 2026 from vendor pages and published comparisons (sources below); this market is barely two years old and pricing moves quickly, so verify before you buy.
